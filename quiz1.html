Q1 Proof by Strong Induction 5 pts
Let \( f_n \) denote the \( n \)-th Fibonacci number defined by \( f_0 = 0 \), \( f_1 = 1 \), and \( f_n = f_{n-1} + f_{n-2} \) for \( n \geq 2 \).

Prove by strong induction that for all \( n \geq 1 \): \[ f_n < \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^n \]
Solution: We prove \( f_n < \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^n \) for all \( n \geq 1 \) by strong induction.

Base Cases: \( f_1 = 1 < \frac{7}{4} \) and \( f_2=1 < \frac{49}{16} \). Both hold.

Inductive Step: Assume \( f_k < \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^k \) for all \( 1 \leq k \leq n \). Then: \[ \begin{aligned} f_{n+1} &=f_n + f_{n-1} < \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^n + \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n-1}\\ &=\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{7}{4} + 1\right)=\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n-1} \cdot \frac{11}{4} \end{aligned} \] Since \( \frac{11}{4}=\frac{44}{16} < \frac{49}{16}=\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^2 \), we have: \[ f_{n+1} < \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n-1} \cdot \left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^2=\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n+1} \]

Q3 Logical Equivalence with Quantifiers 5 pts
Prove or disprove: For predicates \( P(x) \) and \( Q(x) \) over domain \( D \), the following are logically equivalent: \[ \neg(\forall x\, P(x) \to \exists x\, Q(x)) \equiv \forall x\, P(x) \land \forall x\, \neg Q(x) \] If they are equivalent, provide a formal derivation using logical equivalence laws. If not, provide a counterexample with a specific domain and predicates.
Solution: The statements are logically equivalent.

Derivation: \[ \begin{aligned} \neg(\forall x\, P(x) \to \exists x\, Q(x)) &\equiv \neg(\neg\forall x\, P(x) \lor \exists x\, Q(x))\\ &\equiv \forall x\, P(x) \land \neg\exists x\, Q(x)\\ &\equiv \forall x\, P(x) \land \forall x\, \neg Q(x) \end{aligned} \] using the equivalences: \( A \to B \equiv \neg A \lor B \), De Morgan's law, and \( \neg\exists x\, Q(x) \equiv \forall x\, \neg Q(x) \).
Q4 Structural Induction on Regular Expressions 5 pts
Let \( \Sigma = \{a, b\} \). For a regular expression \( R \) over \( \Sigma \), define \( \#_a(R) \) as the number of occurrences of the symbol \( a \) in \( R \) (counting \( a \) in the regex syntax, not in the language).

Prove by structural induction that for any regular expressions \( R \) and \( S \): \[ \#_a(R + S) = \#_a(R) + \#_a(S) \] \[ \#_a(R \cdot S) = \#_a(R) + \#_a(S) \] \[ \#_a(R^*) = \#_a(R) \]
Solution: We prove by structural induction on the construction of regular expressions.

Base Cases: For atomic regex \( \emptyset, \varepsilon, a, b \), the properties hold trivially (count the \( a \)'s directly).

Inductive Cases: Assume properties hold for \( R \) and \( S \).

Union: \( \#_a(R + S) \) counts all \( a \)'s appearing in the syntax of \( R \) plus all in \( S \), giving \( \#_a(R) + \#_a(S) \).

Concatenation: \( \#_a(R \cdot S) \) counts all \( a \)'s in \( R \) and \( S \) without adding new ones, giving \( \#_a(R) + \#_a(S) \).

Kleene Star: \( \#_a(R^*) \) adds only the \( * \) symbol to \( R \), preserving the count: \( \#_a(R^*) = \#_a(R) \).
Q5 Divisibility and Proof by Cases 5 pts
Prove that for any integer \( n \), the expression \( n^5 - n \) is divisible by 30.

Hint: Factor the expression and use proof by cases considering \( n \bmod 2, 3, 5 \).
Solution: Factor: \( n^5 - n = n(n-1)(n+1)(n^2+1) \).

Since \( 30 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \), we verify divisibility by each prime:

By 2: Among three consecutive integers \( n-1, n, n+1 \), one is even.

By 3: Considering \( n \bmod 3 \):
  • \( n \equiv 0 \): then \( 3 \mid n \)
  • \( n \equiv 1 \): then \( 3 \mid (n-1) \)
  • \( n \equiv 2 \): then \( 3 \mid (n+1) \)

By 5: Considering \( n \bmod 5 \):
  • \( n \equiv 0 \): then \( 5 \mid n \)
  • \( n \equiv \pm 1 \): then \( 5 \mid (n \mp 1) \)
  • \( n \equiv \pm 2 \): then \( n^2 \equiv 4 \pmod{5} \), so \( 5 \mid (n^2+1) \)

Therefore \( 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30 \) divides \( n^5 - n \).

Q7 Resolution Proof System 5 pts
Using only the Resolution rule (from \( p \lor q \) and \( \neg p \lor r \), infer \( q \lor r \)), prove that the following set of clauses is unsatisfiable: \[ \{p \lor q, \, \neg p \lor r, \, \neg q \lor s, \, \neg r \lor s, \, \neg s\} \] Show each resolution step clearly, indicating which clauses are being resolved.
Solution: Given: \( \{p \lor q,\, \neg p \lor r,\, \neg q \lor s,\, \neg r \lor s,\, \neg s\} \)

\[ \begin{aligned} (1) \land (2) &\implies q \lor r && \text{(resolve on } p) \\ (q \lor r) \land (3) &\implies r \lor s && \text{(resolve on } q) \\ (r \lor s) \land (4) &\implies s && \text{(resolve on } r) \\ s \land (5) &\implies \square && \text{(resolve on } s) \end{aligned} \] The empty clause proves unsatisfiability.
